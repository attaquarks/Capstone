Grounding Justification: Why Our Agent Needs Domain-Specific Data
====================================================================

The Supply Chain Intelligence Agent cannot rely solely on the LLM's pre-trained
knowledge for the following critical reasons:

1. PRIVATE & PROPRIETARY DATA: Our inventory levels, supplier contracts, pricing
   tiers, and procurement history are internal company data that no public LLM has
   ever seen. The LLM cannot know that "PRD-001 has 150 units in stock" without
   access to our live inventory report. Without grounding, the agent would either
   hallucinate stock levels or refuse to answer.

2. REAL-TIME ACCURACY: Supply chain data changes daily. Lead times shift, stock
   depletes, new shipments arrive. The LLM's training data is static and months
   old. By grounding the agent in our vector database (updated via ingest_data.py),
   we ensure every response reflects the current state of operations.

3. DOMAIN-SPECIFIC CALCULATIONS: Reorder risk scores depend on the specific
   relationship between current_stock, reorder_point, lead_time_days, and
   supplier_reliability_score. These formulas are proprietary to our operations
   and cannot be inferred by a general-purpose LLM.

4. SUPPLIER RELATIONSHIPS: Each supplier has unique payment terms, minimum order
   values, certifications, and reliability scores that directly impact procurement
   decisions. The LLM has no knowledge of "SUP-101 BearingTech Industries" or
   their Net 30 payment terms.

5. COMPLIANCE & TRACEABILITY: In supply chain management, every recommendation
   must be traceable to source data. RAG provides this traceability through
   metadata (source_file, doc_type, last_updated), enabling auditors to verify
   that the agent's suggestions are grounded in actual company records rather
   than LLM speculation.

In summary, the RAG pipeline transforms our agent from a generic chatbot into a
domain-specific decision support system that supply chain managers can trust for
operational decisions involving real money and real inventory.
